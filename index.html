The four ways an AI-built app quietly costs you money

  1. Database rules left wide open. If Row Level Security (Supabase) or your Firebase rules aren't doing their job, anyone holding your public anon key — which ships in your frontend — can read or write every table. Your users' data, gone or changed, no hacking required.
  2. Server keys shipped to the browser. A service_role or secret API key inside a frontend bundle bypasses every rule you wrote. It's a master key, published.
  3. AI endpoints with no limits. Routes that call paid APIs (chat, image, voice) without rate limits or spend caps can be scripted overnight — you find out from the invoice.
  4. Public storage buckets. If bucket policies let the public role list files, every "private" upload your users trusted you with is enumerable by anyone.

Every finding comes with file-and-line evidence and a fix prompt you can paste straight into Cursor (or hand to your AI builder) — no security background needed to apply it.

What this is not

Is this done by AI?
Analysis is AI-assisted; every single finding is re-verified against your actual code by a human reviewer before it reaches the report — if we can't point at the line, it doesn't go in.

I use Supabase Auth (or Firebase Auth). Isn't security already handled?
Auth handles who signs in. It doesn't decide what a signed-in — or anonymous — visitor can read and write. That's your database rules, your policies, and where your keys live: exactly the parts the AI generates and nobody reviews. Most of what we find lives in that gap.
